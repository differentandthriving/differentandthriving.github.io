Working toward your goals

Coaching

You're starting in a place of overwhelm. That's okay. The world doesn't feel like it's made for you. But you can still have your goals, your way forward, your way to thrive.

As a coach, my aim is to listen and ask questions that help you as a neurodivergent client or family member think through the goals that are important to you. I will be listening closely and asking a lot of questions to help you find these goals and the best way of working toward them, using the strengths and abilities you already have. My role isn't to give advice or direct, because you as the client know your own life better than anybody. My role is to support and facilitate.

At the same time, as a neurodivergent person and parent myself, I can bring resources and tools to the table that you can pick up and use to help yourself thrive.

Meet your coach

Tiffany

Growing up, I often felt isolated, but I didn't know why. I just knew I was different and didn't fit in. Later, as an adult, I found out I had dyslexia and started to reinterpret the challenges I faced. I began to recognize that the mind that made me unique also came with untold strengths, which I had been using all along. Just a few years later, my son was diagnosed with autism. As a mom, I wondered how to support him, but I knew he would have his own set of strengths to lean on. His journey is and will be so different from mine, because he already knows where his strengths are and has tools to face his challenges.

I want to help kids recognize their strengths earlier than I did, and I want to support parents in the overwhelm while helping them use the skills they already have to step toward achievable goals.
